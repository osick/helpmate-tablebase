# Authorship

**These positions were enumerated. They were not composed.**

This project solves whole material classes exhaustively. For every legal
position in a class it stores the distance to mate under optimal cooperation
and the number of distinct optimal solutions. The showcase then lists, per
class, the deepest positions whose solution is unique and the deepest whose
two solutions differ in both their first and last move.

Nothing in that process involves a choice. No position here was selected for
its beauty, and none was arrived at by having an idea and looking for a
setting to express it. A machine read every cell of a table and reported the
extremes. That is a measurement, and this page exists to keep it from being
mistaken for anything else.

## What this is not

The Codex of Chess Compositions, the rules of the world federation for chess
composition, addresses this case directly. Article 2, footnote 3:

> The use of a computer does not result in an authorship of the computer. Nor
> does the compilation or publication of a computer generated database
> constitute the publication of one or more chess compositions.

So: publishing this corpus is not publishing compositions. No authorship is
claimed here — not by the program, not by its author, not by this project.

## It does not anticipate anyone

Article 24(1):

> A chess composition is anticipated if there is an identical composition
> which has an earlier priority date. The anticipated chess composition is not
> eligible for any award.

Anticipation requires an earlier **composition**. Footnote 3 says a generated
database is not one. A composer who arrives independently at a position that
also appears in this corpus is not anticipated by it, and their work remains
eligible on its own merits. This corpus creates no priority dates and takes
nothing off the table.

## What this project will not do

The concern the composition world has actually raised about tablebase mining
is not that the data exists. It is that a person can mine a table, take what
falls out, and enter it in a composing tourney as their own work, with little
or no creative input.

This project will not do that. Nothing found here has been or will be entered
in any composing tourney by its author, under any name. If you use this corpus
in your own composing, what you enter should be yours: the idea, the setting,
the reason the position is worth looking at.

## Credit, where a position is already known

Some positions in this corpus are already published compositions, found long
ago by people who did the real work. Those are crossed against a database of
published problems and carry their author and source wherever a match is
found; 26 are credited so far.

That list is certainly incomplete. **If you recognise a position as yours or
as someone else's, please open an issue** — corrections and additions to the
attribution are the most useful contribution anyone can make to this
repository, and they will be applied.

## What it is good for

The useful direction is the reverse of the one people fear. An exhaustive
table answers questions a composer otherwise has to take on trust:

- Is my problem **sound** — is the solution really unique, or is there a dual?
- Is it the **deepest** of its kind in this material, or is there a longer one?
- How many positions in this class share the property I think is rare?

That is the same service an anticipation database provides, extended from
"has anyone published this?" to "does the position have the property I claim?"
The corpus is a verification instrument. It is meant to sit alongside a
composer's work, not in front of it.

## The part the rules do not settle

A tablebase can establish that a position is the deepest of its kind and that
its solution is unique. It cannot establish that the position is any good, and
nothing here claims that it is. Depth and uniqueness are measurements.
Economy, surprise, the quality of the mating picture, whether an idea has been
expressed before and expressed better — those are judgements, and they belong
to composers and judges.

Where this corpus does report quality, it reports only defects it can check
mechanically: whether the diagram is legal, whether the side to move is
already in check, whether the solution opens with a capture. Those are
disqualifiers, not merits. The absence of a flag is not a claim of beauty.

There is a real asymmetry here, and it is worth naming rather than
glossing over: a composer may spend weeks on a single problem that appears in
this corpus as one row among hundreds. What the enumeration does not do is
make that work redundant. It finds positions; it does not find ideas, and it
cannot tell which of the positions it found is worth anyone's attention. That
judgement is exactly the part it cannot automate, and it is the part the art
consists of.
